How it works

Every waveform setting the radar can transmit repeats almost exactly from pulse to pulse. So instead of training a network, we averaged the 80 training pulses of each of the 660 settings into one template, and the model just asks which template the incoming pulse lines up with best.

The dots on the scope are those 660 templates, inside their modulation’s segment: the brighter the dot, the better the pulse lines up with it.

What it can’t do

  • It only knows the 660 settings in the dataset. Hide a pulse’s own setting and accuracy falls to 0.48, so a waveform it has never heard will be named wrongly with confidence.
  • The spectrogram is here for you. The model reads time-domain I/Q only; this is our Track A entry, which allows no FFT anywhere.
  • Noise is the organizers’ rule: complex Gaussian noise sized from the power in fixed windows, then rounded back to 16-bit samples, exactly as in the competition.
